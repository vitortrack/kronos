# Verification, selection and critique techniques in coding agents: measured effects

Scope: best-of-N with test- or verifier-based selection, LLM critics and judges, execution feedback in the loop, and cost/benefit. Research date: 2026-11-09. Each number gives its date, model, benchmark and N where the source states them. Items marked "(abstract, not re-fetched)" are well-known paper abstracts I am citing from prior knowledge and did not re-open in this session. Treat their exact wording as needing a check.

TrackAI baseline for comparison (internal, not a published source): blind reviewer 60% -> 60%; best-of-3 picked by tests passing + smallest diff 60% -> 90% at ~2.6x tokens.

## 1. Best-of-N / parallel sampling with test- or verifier-based selection: size of gains, scaling with N, gap lost to imperfect selectors

### Takeaway
On SWE-bench-style tasks, parallel sampling with execution-based filtering plus a scorer adds about +5 to +8 points for frontier models (Anthropic, 2025). Oracle coverage keeps growing log-linearly with N. Real selectors recover only about 25-50% of the gap between random pick and oracle (pass@N). The best selectors are hybrids: visible or regression tests first, then a model or trained critic ranks what survives. Neither tests alone nor an LLM judge alone does as well. With imperfect verifiers (weak tests), returns peak at a small N (often under 10) and can turn negative.

### Cited Findings
**Frontier vendor reports (SWE-bench Verified, "parallel test-time compute")**
- Claude 3.7 Sonnet (Feb 2025): 63.7% standard -> 70.3% "high compute" (+6.6 pp), on a 489-task subset. Method: sample multiple parallel attempts; discard patches that break the repo's *visible regression tests* ("similar to the rejection sampling approach adopted by Agentless", no hidden tests); rank the rest with a scoring model and submit the best. The number of attempts is not disclosed. — [Anthropic, Claude 3.7 Sonnet](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-3-7-sonnet)
- Claude Opus 4 (May 2025): 72.5% -> 79.4% (+6.9 pp). Claude Sonnet 4: 72.7% -> 80.2% (+7.5 pp). Same method: parallel attempts, regression-test rejection, internal scoring model. N not disclosed. — [Anthropic, Claude 4](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-4)
- Claude Sonnet 4.5 (Sep 2025): 77.2% (avg of 10 trials, full 500) -> 82.0% with parallel test-time compute (+4.8 pp). Same method. N not disclosed. — [Anthropic, Claude Sonnet 4.5](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-sonnet-4-5)

**Coverage (pass@N) scaling**
- Large Language Monkeys (Jul 2024): DeepSeek-Coder-V2-Instruct on SWE-bench Lite. Issues solved by at least one sample go from 15.9% at 1 sample to 56% at 250 samples; single-sample SOTA at the time was 43%. Coverage scales log-linearly over 4 orders of magnitude. In domains without automatic verifiers, majority voting and reward models "plateau beyond several hundred samples" and "fail to fully scale with the sample budget". — [Brown et al., arXiv 2407.21787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21787)

**Gap between oracle (pass@N) and selected@N: worked examples**
- Agentless (Jul 2024; GPT-4o; SWE-bench Lite, 300 issues; 40 candidate patches per issue = 4 location sets x 10 samples). Selection ablation: majority voting over AST-normalized patches gives 25.67%; + regression tests gives 27.00%; + LLM-generated reproduction tests (40 per issue, 1 greedy + 39 sampled) gives 32.00%. Oracle "any sample correct" = 42.0%, so 10 pp is lost to selection. Reproduction tests were weak: of 300 issues, 213 got a test that reproduced the bug on the original repo, and only 94 also passed with the ground-truth patch. Performance plateaus at ~40 patch samples. — [Xia et al., Agentless, arXiv 2407.01489](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01489)
- CodeMonkeys (Jan 2025; Claude 3.5 Sonnet 20241022 + a local Qwen2.5-Coder-32B for file relevance; SWE-bench Verified; 10 test+edit state machines per issue, max 8 iterations each). Oracle coverage 69.8%; expected random selection 45.8%; their selection (voting on model-generated tests, keep top 3, then a model "selection state machine") gives 57.4%. That is 12.4 pp below oracle, recovering ~half the random-to-oracle gap. "Model selection without filtering underperforms majority voting; with top-3 filtering it outperforms it." — [Ehrlich et al., CodeMonkeys, arXiv 2501.14723](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14723)
- CodeMonkeys "Barrel of Monkeys" ensemble (CodeMonkeys + top-4 leaderboard submissions; 5 candidates per issue): coverage 80.8%, random 60.9%, selected 66.2%. That beats the best single member (62.8%) but recovers only ~27% of the random-to-oracle gap. — [arXiv 2501.14723](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14723)
- OpenAI o1-ioi at IOI 2024 (paper Feb 2025): 10,000 samples per subtask; clustering by outputs on 256 model-generated test inputs; reranking with a learned scoring function plus generated-test and public-test failures. With 50 submissions: 213 pts (49th percentile). Random 50 would score ~156 pts. With 10,000 submissions (no selection): 362.14 pts (gold). Selection recovers ~28% of the gap. o3 (1,024 samples, top 50, self-written brute-force cross-checks that emerged from RL, no hand-built pipeline): 395.64 pts (gold) under contest rules. Codeforces: o1-ioi 1807 base -> 2092 with public-test filtering -> 2214 with the full test-time strategy. — [OpenAI, Competitive Programming with Large Reasoning Models, arXiv 2502.06807](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06807)
- OpenHands critic (Mar 2026): small trajectory-level critic (openhands-critic-4b-v1.0, ~1 s latency), trained on production user-agent interactions with 24 "Critic Rubrics" + sparse outcome proxies (code survival, PR merge). On the *mixed-outcome* subset of SWE-bench Verified (instances where at least one of 8 runs succeeds and at least one fails, so oracle = 100%): Best@8 73.8% vs Random@8 57.9% (+15.9 pp, ~38% of the gap). — [OpenHands blog, Learning to verify AI-generated code](https://openhands.dev/blog/20260305-learning-to-verify-ai-generated-code); [Wang, Chen, Ji, Neubig, arXiv 2603.03800](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03800)
- OpenHands critic v1 (Apr 2025): critic fine-tuned from Qwen2.5-Coder-32B on SWE-Gym rollouts; Claude 3.7 Sonnet agent; SWE-bench Verified 60.6% single rollout -> 66.4% best-of-5 ("log-linear"). The 66.4% figure was seen via the model-card mirror, so treat it with some caution. — [OpenHands blog, SOTA with inference-time scaling and critic model](https://openhands.dev/blog/sota-on-swe-bench-verified-with-inference-time-scaling-and-critic-model); [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/OpenHands/openhands-critic-32b-exp-20250417)

**Verifier type comparisons (open models)**
- R2E-Gym (Apr 2025; 32B open agent; SWE-bench Verified): pass@1 34.4%. Execution-based (test) verifiers and execution-free (LLM/trained judge) verifiers each saturate at ~42-43% best@k. A hybrid of both reaches 51%. Failure modes: test-based verifiers have "low distinguishability" (many candidates pass or fail the same tests); execution-free verifiers "are biased and often rely on stylistic features". — [Jain et al., R2E-Gym, arXiv 2504.07164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07164)
- SWE-Gym (Dec 2024; Qwen2.5-Coder-32B agent + trained verifier): with verifier-based inference-time scaling, 32.0% on SWE-bench Verified and 26.0% on Lite. A secondary summary gives +11.4 pp absolute from the verifier; not confirmed on the abstract page. — [Pan et al., SWE-Gym, arXiv 2412.21139](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.21139)
- Trae Agent (Jul 2025): generator / pruner / selector agents; SWE-bench Verified 75.20% (claimed #1 at the time); +10.22% average Pass@1 over four ensemble-reasoning baselines across three LLMs. Per-N and oracle numbers are not in the abstract. — [Trae Agent, arXiv 2507.23370](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.23370)
- S* (Feb 2025; LiveCodeBench; 12 models): parallel sampling + sequential refinement, plus selection by *adaptively synthesized distinguishing inputs* executed for pairwise comparison of candidates. Results: a 3B model beats GPT-4o-mini; GPT-4o-mini + S* beats o1-preview by 3.7 pp; R1-Distill-Qwen-32B + S* reaches 85.7% vs o1 (high) 88.5%. — [Li et al., S*, arXiv 2502.14382](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14382)

**Older function-level selection results (pre-2023 / 2023, for scale)**
- CodeT (2022): code-davinci-002 generates tests and code; selection by dual execution agreement. HumanEval pass@1 rises to 65.8%, +18.8 pp over the base model. — [Chen et al., CodeT, arXiv 2207.10397](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.10397) (abstract, not re-fetched)
- LEVER (2023): a learned verifier over NL + program + execution result reranks samples. Gains of 4.6 to 10.9 pp over code-davinci-002 on Spider, WikiTQ, GSM8K and MBPP. — [Ni et al., LEVER, arXiv 2302.08468](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08468) (abstract, not re-fetched)
- AlphaCode (2022): up to millions of samples; filtering by example tests removes ~99%; clustering picks 10 submissions; average Codeforces ranking in the top 54.3%. — [Li et al., arXiv 2203.07814](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.07814) (abstract, not re-fetched)
- DEI (Aug 2024): a committee of open SWE agents whose best member scores 27.3% on SWE-bench Lite reaches 34.3% when an LLM reviewer picks among their patches. — [Zhang et al., Diversity Empowers Intelligence, arXiv 2408.07060](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07060) (abstract, not re-fetched)

**Ceiling from imperfect verifiers**
- Stroebl, Kapoor, Narayanan (Nov 2024; HumanEval, MBPP): resampling until unit tests pass has an accuracy ceiling set by the verifier's false-positive rate "regardless of compute budget". Model single-sample accuracy correlates strongly with its false-positive rate. Scaling a weaker model cannot match a sufficiently stronger model's single-sample accuracy. The optimal number of attempts is "often fewer than 10", after which false positives make curves bend downward. False-positive passes also lower code quality (style conventions). — [Stroebl et al., Inference Scaling fLaws, arXiv 2411.17501](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17501)
- Test-passing does not mean correct on SWE-bench itself (Mar 2025): 7.8% of patches counted as resolved fail the full developer test suite. 29.6% of plausible patches behave differently from the ground truth; 28.6% of those divergent patches are certainly incorrect. 27.3% of divergences come from patches that change *more* behavior than the ground truth. Reported resolve rates are inflated by 6.2 pp absolute. — [Wang et al., Are "Solved Issues" in SWE-bench Really Solved Correctly?, arXiv 2503.15223](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15223)

### Inferences
- Gap recovered by real selectors, computed from the cited numbers: Agentless (majority vote to oracle) 10.0 pp lost of 16.3; CodeMonkeys ~48% of the random-to-oracle gap recovered; Barrel ~27%; o1-ioi ~28%; OpenHands 2026 critic ~38%. As a rule of thumb, expect a well-built selector to capture a quarter to a half of (pass@N minus random), not most of it.
- TrackAI's 60% -> 90% from best-of-3 is far above published gains (+5 to +8 pp at frontier, ~+6 pp for best-of-5 with a trained critic). Likely causes: a small or noisy task set; selection tests that are, or overlap with, the acceptance tests (which measures pass@3, not selected@3); or a weak base where many tasks are "flaky-solvable". Before trusting it, check that the selection tests are hidden from the scorer, or run on a larger held-out set.
- Published systems all use the same two stages: (1) reject by execution (visible regression tests, generated reproduction tests, lint), then (2) rank survivors with a scorer (trained critic, LLM selection agent, or test voting). TrackAI's "tests pass + smallest diff" is stage 1 with a heuristic tie-breaker. The smallest-diff heuristic has weak indirect support: in 27.3% of divergent plausible patches the patch over-changes behavior ([arXiv 2503.15223](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15223)). It has not been measured as a selector.
- Gains shrink as base accuracy rises (Anthropic: +6.6/+6.9/+7.5 -> +4.8 pp). For a weaker open model like glm-5.3-flash, absolute gains from N may be larger, but the Stroebl result says to keep N small (<10) when the tests are weak.
- When the repo has no visible tests for the change, the reproduction-test approach (Agentless, CodeMonkeys) is the documented substitute. Its weakness is measured: only 94 of 300 generated reproduction tests were valid in both directions in Agentless.

### Gaps
- Anthropic never disclosed N or the scoring model for its parallel-compute results, so I could not measure gain per N for frontier models.
- I found no published SWE-bench result for "tests pass + smallest diff" selection specifically.
- I did not find R2E-Gym's or Trae Agent's exact best@k curves or oracle pass@k (abstract only).
- I found no 2026 vendor report (OpenAI, Google, Anthropic) with parallel-compute ablations newer than Sonnet 4.5. I also could not confirm whether later models stopped reporting the metric.
- I found no data on GLM-family models with these selection methods.

## 2. LLM critics, reviewers and judges for code: when they help, fail, or are biased

### Takeaway
A prompted LLM critic that sees only the candidate (no execution evidence, no alternatives) has no reliable positive evidence. Intrinsic self-correction often *lowers* accuracy, debate gains come from voting, and LLM code judges are swayed by surface features and answer order. Critics help in three settings: (a) trained critics (RL-trained, or trained on real outcomes) used to *rank multiple candidates*; (b) critics given execution signals or used after test-based filtering; (c) feedback from a stronger model or a human. TrackAI's blind reviewer result (60% -> 60%) matches this literature.

### Cited Findings
**Intrinsic self-correction / prompted critics**
- Huang et al. (Oct 2023, ICLR 2024). Intrinsic self-correction (no external feedback), standard -> round 1 -> round 2: GPT-3.5 GSM8K 75.9 -> 75.1 -> 74.7; GPT-3.5 CommonSenseQA 75.8 -> 38.1 -> 41.8; GPT-4 GSM8K 95.5 -> 91.5 -> 89.0; Llama-2 GSM8K 62.0 -> 43.5 -> 36.5. The earlier reported gains came from *oracle labels* (stopping only when the answer is wrong): GPT-3.5 GSM8K 75.9 -> 84.3 with oracle. The model is more likely to change a correct answer to an incorrect one than the reverse. — [Huang et al., LLMs Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet, arXiv 2310.01798](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.01798v2)
- Same paper, multi-agent debate vs self-consistency at equal responses (GSM8K, GPT-3.5): debate 83.2 (6 responses) vs SC 85.3 (6); debate 83.0 (9) vs SC 88.2 (9). — [arXiv 2310.01798](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.01798v2)
- Debate or Vote (Aug 2025, NeurIPS 2025 spotlight; 7 NLP benchmarks): majority voting "alone accounts for most of the performance gains" credited to multi-agent debate. They prove debate induces a martingale over beliefs, i.e. it does not improve expected correctness; "simple ensembling remains a strong and more reliable option". — [Choi, Zhu, Li, arXiv 2508.17536](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.17536)
- Kamoi et al. survey (2024, TACL): self-correction works when reliable external feedback exists (e.g. execution). There is no evidence of success with feedback from prompted LLMs, except on tasks unusually suited to it. — [arXiv 2406.01297](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01297) (abstract, not re-fetched)
- Tyen et al. (Nov 2023): LLMs are poor at *finding* the location of reasoning errors but can correct them once given the location. Error-locating is the bottleneck. — [arXiv 2311.08516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08516) (abstract, not re-fetched)

**Self-repair / reflection on code**
- Olausson et al. (ICLR 2024; Code Llama, GPT-3.5, GPT-4; HumanEval, APPS). At an *equal sample budget*, self-repair is often no better than i.i.d. resampling. GPT-4 APPS: 10 initial + 1 repair each = 1.05x the pass@20 baseline; 2 initial + 10 repairs each = 0.97x pass@22. Feedback quality is the bottleneck: GPT-4 repair success with GPT-4's own feedback was 33.3%, with human feedback 52.6% (1.58x). By difficulty, introductory 42.6 -> 62.2, interview 19.3 -> 45.7, competition 3.7 -> 14.7. Feedback from a stronger model beat both baseline and self-repair at all budgets. Recommendation: favor diverse initial samples over many repair rounds; the best setting was 1 repair per initial sample. — [Olausson et al., Is Self-Repair a Silver Bullet?, arXiv 2306.09896](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.09896)
- Reflexion (2023, GPT-4). HumanEval-Py 80.1 -> 91.0 pass@1, but MBPP-Py 80.1 -> 77.1 (a *drop*), explained by self-generated tests with a 16.3% false-positive rate on MBPP vs 1.4% on HumanEval. Ablation on 50 hard HumanEval-Rust problems: base 0.60; without test generation 0.52; without self-reflection 0.60; full 0.68. Reflection text alone added nothing; gains needed executed tests. — [Shinn et al., Reflexion, arXiv 2303.11366](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11366)

**Trained critics**
- CriticGPT / "LLM Critics Help Catch LLM Bugs" (OpenAI, Jun 2024; RLHF-trained critic on code). Model critiques were preferred over human critiques in 63% of cases on naturally occurring LLM errors, and models catch more bugs than paid human contractors. But critics "hallucinate bugs"; human+model teams catch a similar number of bugs while hallucinating less. — [McAleese et al., arXiv 2407.00215](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00215)
- OpenHands critic (Mar 2026). A critic trained only on benchmark-style data scores AUC ~0.45-0.48 on real production outcomes (worse than random). Production supervision gives AUC 0.58 (PR-merge proxy) and 0.69 (code-survival proxy). The same critic used to *rank 8 rollouts* gives +15.9 pp over random on SWE-bench Verified mixed-outcome instances. — [OpenHands blog](https://openhands.dev/blog/20260305-learning-to-verify-ai-generated-code)
- CodeMonkeys (Jan 2025): an LLM selection agent *without* test filtering underperforms plain majority voting over tests; *after* top-3 test filtering it beats voting. — [arXiv 2501.14723](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14723)
- R2E-Gym (Apr 2025): execution-free verifiers saturate at ~42-43% and "rely on stylistic features"; combining them with execution-based ones gives 51%. — [arXiv 2504.07164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07164)

**Biases of LLM code judges**
- "Don't Judge Code by Its Cover" (May 2025, EACL 2026 Findings; 5 languages, multiple LLMs). "All tested LLM judges are susceptible to both positive and negative biases" from surface variations (variable names, comments, formatting). Prompting the judge to write test cases first does not fully fix this. — [arXiv 2505.16222](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16222)
- CodeJudgeBench (Jul 2025, ACL 2026; 26 judges; code generation, repair, test generation). Thinking models judge much better than non-thinking ones, but "all models still exhibit significant randomness". Accuracy depends on response position; a secondary summary reports up to ~11 pp position sensitivity (not verified in the paper). — [Jiang et al., arXiv 2507.10535](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.10535)
- Self-preference: LLM evaluators recognize their own generations and favor them; self-recognition strength correlates linearly with self-preference. — [Panickssery et al., arXiv 2404.13076](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076) (abstract, not re-fetched)

### Inferences
- TrackAI's blind reviewer is a prompted, execution-free critic gating a *single* candidate, followed by revision. That is the configuration with the worst evidence: intrinsic critique/self-repair (Huang, Olausson), surface-feature bias (2505.16222), and probably same-model self-preference if the reviewer is also glm-5.3-flash. "No change" in pass rate is the expected result, not an implementation failure.
- Better uses of the same reviewer tokens, ordered by strength of evidence: (1) use it as a selector over best-of-N survivors *after* test filtering, which helps per CodeMonkeys, R2E-Gym's hybrid and Anthropic's method; (2) give it execution evidence (test output, diff of behavior on generated inputs, à la S*/CodeT) instead of the diff alone; (3) if it stays a gate, run it with a stronger or different model (Olausson: stronger-model feedback beats self-feedback), and measure its false-reject rate. With 2 revision rounds, it can turn correct patches into wrong ones (Huang: correct -> incorrect flips outnumber the reverse).
- Pairwise comparison should be order-randomized or run in both orders (position bias).
- Debate or multiple reviewers voting gives no gain over simply sampling more candidates and voting.

### Gaps
- I found no controlled study of a "blind diff reviewer" gate inside a SWE-bench agent, with and without the reviewer, on the same base agent. Systems that report reviewers fold them into selection.
- CriticGPT's exact bug-catch rates and hallucination rates were not in the abstract; I did not get the paper body.
- I found no measurements of GLM-family models as code judges.
- The position-bias magnitude for CodeJudgeBench comes from a secondary summary.

## 3. Execution feedback in the loop (tests, linters, type checkers, error messages)

### Takeaway
Gating edits on a linter or syntax check and feeding tests back are the most consistently positive in-loop signals, but the measured effects are moderate: about +3 pp from SWE-agent's linter, and +2-12 pp for Self-Debugging with unit tests. Self-generated tests help only if they are valid; invalid tests make things worse. Most of the value of retry-until-plausible loops comes in the first 1-2 attempts.

### Cited Findings
- SWE-agent (May 2024; GPT-4 Turbo; SWE-bench Lite subset). Full ACI 18.0% resolved; edit action *without linting* 15.0% (-3.0); no edit command 10.3% (-7.7). Other ACI choices mattered as much: iterative search 12.0 (-6.0); full-file viewer 12.7 (-5.3); 30-line viewer 14.3 (-3.7); full history instead of last-5 observations 15.0 (-3.0). 51.7% of trajectories had at least one failed edit. The chance an edit eventually succeeds falls from ~90.5% to ~57.2% after a single failed edit. — [Yang et al., SWE-agent, arXiv 2405.15793](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15793)
- Aider (May 2024; SWE-bench Lite; GPT-4o and Opus alternating, up to 6 fresh attempts until "plausible" = edits apply, no lint errors, no failing pre-existing or new tests; acceptance tests hidden). Attempt 1 (GPT-4o) gives 20.3%; all 6 attempts give 26.3%. Of 79 resolved, 61 came on attempt 1 and 71 within 2 attempts; attempts 3-6 added only 8 (2.7 pp). No ablation of lint or test feedback alone was published. — [Aider blog](https://aider.chat/2024/05/22/swe-bench-lite.html)
- Self-Debugging (Apr 2023). Text-to-SQL without unit tests: +2-3%, and +9% on the hardest Spider problems. TransCoder and MBPP with unit tests: up to +12%. — [Chen et al., arXiv 2304.05128](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05128) (abstract, not re-fetched)
- Reflexion (2023). Removing self-generated test execution dropped HumanEval-Rust-hard from base 0.60 to 0.52; reflection without tests = no gain (0.60). A 16.3% false-positive rate in self-tests caused a net loss on MBPP-Py (80.1 -> 77.1). — [arXiv 2303.11366](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11366)
- Agentless (Jul 2024). Running regression tests to filter adds +1.33 pp over majority voting; LLM-generated reproduction tests add another +5 pp. — [arXiv 2407.01489](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01489)
- o1-ioi (2025). Public-test filtering alone raised Codeforces rating from 1807 to 2092; the full strategy (generated test inputs, clustering, learned scorer) reached 2214. o3 learned to write brute-force reference solutions and cross-check against them on its own. — [arXiv 2502.06807](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06807)
- Type-constrained decoding (Apr 2025; TypeScript; HumanEval/MBPP; synthesis, translation, repair; open models up to >30B). Compilation errors are reduced by more than half, and functional correctness improves (exact number not in the abstract). This requires decoder access, which hosted APIs usually do not allow. — [Mündler et al., arXiv 2504.09246](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09246)
- Over-trusting tests (Mar 2025). Even official SWE-bench tests let 7.8% of incorrect patches through, inflating scores by 6.2 pp. — [arXiv 2503.15223](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15223)

### Inferences
- TrackAI's syntax check before apply is consistent with SWE-agent's linter gate (+3 pp, and it prevents failed-edit cascades). The cascade data argues for blocking a broken edit and returning the error immediately, since one failed edit drops eventual success from ~90% to ~57%.
- TrackAI's "reflection nudge after 3 failing calls" has no direct measurement. The evidence (Huang, Reflexion ablation, Olausson) suggests reflection text adds little unless it carries new external information (the error output or test diff). An earlier trigger (after 1-2 failures, given the 90.5% -> 57.2% drop) or a fresh restart (Aider-style, Olausson's "more initial samples") may be worth testing.
- Generated reproduction tests are worth it for selection only if checked in both directions (fail before, pass after the patch). Agentless shows only ~31% (94/300) were valid both ways.

### Gaps
- I found no quantitative study of "teaching the fix" error-message wording (error messages that say how to fix) in agent tools. Anthropic and others give qualitative guidance only. SWE-agent's linter message is the closest measured case.
- I found no published ablation of type-checker feedback (tsc/mypy) as an in-loop agent tool on SWE-bench.
- Execution-guided decoding (line-by-line execution during generation) needs decoder control; I did not collect numbers because it is not applicable through Ollama cloud.

## 4. Cost/benefit: tokens and latency vs accuracy

### Takeaway
Parallel sampling costs roughly linear in N (edit generation dominates), while selection is cheap: ~6% of total cost in CodeMonkeys, $0.25/issue for test-based selection in Agentless. Early stopping with a good critic recovers most of the benefit at a fraction of N. The returns are front-loaded: the first 1-2 extra attempts give most of the gain, and with weak verifiers N should stay below ~10.

### Cited Findings
- CodeMonkeys (Jan 2025; SWE-bench Verified, 500 issues; Claude 3.5 Sonnet + local Qwen 32B). Total ≈ $2,292 (≈ $4.6/issue): edit generation $1,366 (59.6%), test generation $440 (19.2%), context retrieval ~$354 (15.5%), selection $132 (5.8%). Result: 57.4% (random pick from the same candidates would give 45.8%). — [arXiv 2501.14723](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14723)
- Agentless (Jul 2024; GPT-4o; SWE-bench Lite). $0.70/issue on average (78,166 tokens) for the full pipeline with 40 patches; reproduction-test selection costs $0.25/issue for +5 pp over regression-only filtering. — [arXiv 2407.01489](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01489)
- OpenHands critic (Mar 2026). Early stopping (stop when the critic is confident) gives +17.7 pp over random using 1.35 attempts on average vs 8.0, i.e. 83% fewer attempts. Critic latency is under ~1 s per trajectory (4B model). — [OpenHands blog](https://openhands.dev/blog/20260305-learning-to-verify-ai-generated-code); [arXiv 2603.03800](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03800)
- Aider (May 2024). Attempts 1-2 yield 90% of resolved issues (71/79); attempts 3-6 add 2.7 pp, at up to 3x more attempt cost for those instances. — [Aider blog](https://aider.chat/2024/05/22/swe-bench-lite.html)
- Stroebl et al. (Nov 2024). With imperfect unit-test verifiers, the optimal number of resampling attempts is often under 10; beyond that the utility of false positives turns negative. — [arXiv 2411.17501](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17501)
- Olausson et al. (ICLR 2024). At a matched token budget, self-repair ≈ resampling (0.97-1.05x on GPT-4 APPS). Spending budget on more initial samples beats spending it on more repair rounds. — [arXiv 2306.09896](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.09896)
- Agentless also reports a plateau at ~40 patch samples. — [arXiv 2407.01489](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01489)
- At extreme scale, o1-ioi needed 10,000 samples per subtask; o3 replaced this with 1,024 samples and learned self-verification, reaching a higher score (395.64 vs 213 at 50 submissions). — [arXiv 2502.06807](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06807)

### Inferences
- TrackAI's 2.6x tokens for best-of-3 is close to linear cost. Published evidence suggests it can get much of the benefit more cheaply with *sequential early-stopping*: run attempt 1; run attempt 2 only if attempt 1 fails visible tests or the checks; take a 3rd only if both fail. This follows from Aider's front-loading and OpenHands' 1.35-attempt result. It has not been measured in TrackAI.
- The blind reviewer's 2 rounds cost tokens and latency with no measured gain. Moving that budget into selection (ranking surviving candidates) or into generating reproduction tests has measured support (CodeMonkeys, Agentless).
- For a cheaper open model, the Stroebl result implies the selector's false-positive rate, not N, caps accuracy. Investing in test quality (fail-before/pass-after reproduction tests, regression tests) raises the ceiling; raising N past ~5-10 mostly adds cost.

### Gaps
- I found no published latency (wall-clock) vs accuracy curves for parallel SWE agents. Parallel worktrees cut wall-clock relative to sequential retries, but I found no measured numbers.
- Anthropic and OpenAI did not publish token multipliers for their parallel test-time compute SWE-bench results.
- I found no cost data for GLM or Ollama-cloud models with these techniques.
